**Credit risk toolkit: loans, CDS, bonds, Merton:**
Benefits of my Credit Risk Model
- Comprehensive analysis: Combines multiple credit-risk calculations in one Python application.
- Expected Credit Loss (ECL): Calculates potential portfolio losses using PD, LGD and exposure.
- Merton Model: Applies structural credit-risk theory to estimate default probability.
- CDS Analytics: Uses credit default swap spreads to derive implied default risk.
- Bond Valuation: Analyses risky bond prices and credit spread sensitivity.
- Portfolio Management: Allows users to enter and analyse individual loans.
- Sensitivity Analysis: Helps understand how changes in financial assumptions affect risk.

Criticisms and limitations
- Simplified assumptions: Some financial models use approximations rather than full market-calibrated methodologies.
- CDS modelling: Simplified spread-to-hazard calculations may overlook discounting and the full premium and protection legs.
- Model validation: Results need benchmarking against established financial models and market data.

**Black-Scholes Merton Model on Python:**
Benefits of my Black–Scholes Model (BSM)
- Option pricing: Calculates theoretical prices of European call and put options.
- Mathematical application: Demonstrates understanding of stochastic processes and financial mathematics.
- Option Greeks: Can measure sensitivity to underlying price (Delta), volatility (Vega), time (Theta), and interest rates (Rho), if implemented.
- Volatility analysis: Shows how changes in volatility affect option valuations.
- Risk management: Helps assess option exposure and potential changes in portfolio value.
Quantitative finance relevance: Demonstrates exposure to derivatives pricing and financial modelling.

Criticisms and limitations
- European options only: Standard Black–Scholes does not directly price American options with early exercise.
- Constant volatility assumption: Real-world volatility changes over time and across strike prices.
- Constant interest rates: Assumes a fixed risk-free rate over the option's life.
- Lognormal price assumption: Actual market returns can exhibit jumps, skewness and fat tails.
